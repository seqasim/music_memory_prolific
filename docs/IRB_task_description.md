# Music Memory Task — IRB Procedures Description

## Overview

Participants will complete a single-session, browser-based Music Memory task lasting approximately 60 minutes. The task examines how incidental exposure to music during viewing of short video clips relates to later recognition memory for those videos, and how participants subjectively experience the music (familiarity, autobiographical memory, self-relevance, and liking). The session begins with a research-information screen. The three task phases then run in a fixed order: (1) encoding with a simple person-count judgment, (2) recall (old/new recognition with confidence and, after “old” responses, a short source-memory question), and (3) a music survey. The survey follows recognition so that hearing the songs again cannot change memory-test answers. Music stimuli are selected to correspond to a birth-year cohort such that songs fall within the participant’s adolescence and young adulthood (approximately release years *birth year + 15* through *birth year + 30*). Each deployed version of the task serves one music cohort; recruitment is age-screened accordingly.

## Setting and technical requirements

The study is administered remotely over the internet. Participants will use a desktop or laptop computer with a physical keyboard and functioning audio output (headphones or speakers). Mobile phones and tablets are not permitted, because the recognition phase requires left- and right-arrow key responses. Before any task or data collection, participants read a short research-information page and choose to proceed or to decline. Declining ends the session with no completion code and no saved data. Before encoding and again before the music survey, participants will complete a brief audio check in which they hear a spoken word, confirm that they heard it, and type the word; the check repeats until it is completed correctly. Instruction and break screens are self-paced; participants advance by pressing Enter.

## Recruitment and session identifiers

Participants will be recruited through an online research panel or comparable remote recruitment channel. When a panel platform is used, study and participant identifiers supplied by that platform (e.g., participant ID, study ID, and session ID) may be captured from the study URL and recorded with the behavioral data. At the start of the session, participants will provide or confirm their birth year. Birth year is retained in the data record for documentation; the particular music set presented is determined by the cohort for which that instance of the task was prepared and is not changed dynamically by the typed birth year.

## Procedures

### Encoding

During encoding, participants will view 108 short video clips organized into 9 blocks of 12 trials. Within each block, trials are assigned equally to three conditions and then shuffled: no music, music previously associated with music-evoked autobiographical memory (MEAM) in stimulus development, and control music. Each trial begins with a fixation cross (approximately 3 seconds), followed by a short video (approximately 3 seconds). When music is assigned to a trial, the music clip begins at fixation onset and ends when the video ends. While each video plays, participants press **F** if there is one person or fewer in the clip or **J** if there is more than one person (counting people only; the keypress does not end the video). A box appears around the first choice so the selection is visible while the video continues. Participants are also instructed to try to remember the videos. Self-paced instructions appear at the start of each block.

### Recall (old/new recognition)

Following a self-paced break with response instructions, participants will complete a recognition test of 216 video trials (9 blocks of 24). Each block includes the 12 videos from the corresponding encoding block intermixed with 12 never-seen lure videos. Each trial begins with a brief fixation (approximately 750 ms), followed by the video. Participants will press the left arrow key to indicate that the video is old (seen during encoding) or the right arrow key to indicate that it is new. A response may be made during or after the video. After each old/new decision, the chosen response is boxed briefly. Participants then rate confidence by pressing **1** (Sure), **2** (Maybe), or **3** (Guess), and that choice is boxed briefly as well. If they indicated that the video was old, they then answer a source question: when they saw the video, was there no music (**1**), music they knew (**2**), or music they did not know (**3**). That choice is also boxed briefly. Self-paced instructions appear at the start of each recall block.

### Music survey

After the recognition test, participants will hear again each of the 72 music clips that accompanied videos during encoding (36 MEAM and 36 control clips). Each clip is played for approximately 6 seconds (the first portion of the audio file, matching approximate encoding exposure). Immediately afterward, participants answer four closed-ended questions by clicking a number from **1** through **5**: how familiar the song is; whether it brought back memories from their own life (from none to a vivid, specific memory); how much it made them think about themselves; and how much they like it. Each question remains on screen for up to 4 seconds and then advances. A click is boxed briefly. A question left unanswered is recorded as a timeout with no rating. Participants may also leave the study at any time by closing the browser.

## Stimuli

Video stimuli are short, everyday clips drawn from the Memento10k dataset (Newman et al., 2020) and presented without spoken narration as the primary content of the memory task. Music stimuli are short excerpts of commercially released popular music, curated for the target birth-year cohort as described above. Music conditions contrast MEAM and control clips; one third of encoding videos are presented without music. Stimulus assignment (which videos pair with which music conditions, list order, and lure selection) is randomized per participant according to the task’s assignment rules.

## Data collected and storage

The task records trial-level behavioral data in a tidy tabular format (CSV), including whether the participant agreed to the research-information page; phase and trial identifiers; music condition; stimulus file labels; survey question identifiers, responses, and whether a question timed out without an answer; recognition responses (old/new), confidence, and (after old responses) source-memory responses with accuracy coding; encoding person-count judgments with accuracy coding when a label is available; normative video memorability scores from Memento10k as covariates; reaction times; and session metadata such as participant and panel identifiers (when available), birth year, session start time, browser information, screen dimensions, and counts of focus loss as attention-related environment proxies. Data are uploaded to the experiment hosting platform at the end of a completed session. As a safeguard against incomplete remote sessions, trial data may also be retained temporarily in the participant’s browser local storage so that a disrupted session can potentially be recovered. A completion flag distinguishes sessions that reached the end of the timeline from sessions that did not.

Participants do not provide free-text autobiographical narratives; survey items are closed-ended ratings or choices about reactions to the music and videos. No clinical assessments, biometric recordings, or identifiable audiovisual recordings of the participant are collected by the task software.
